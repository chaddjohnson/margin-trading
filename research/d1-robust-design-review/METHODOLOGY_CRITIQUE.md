# Methodology critique of the prior D1 pool research (Phase 1 design review, 2026-10-25)

Scope: the joint 16-ETF study (`research/joint-16-etf-d1/`, frozen 2026-10-25), the portfolio-aware study
(`research/portfolio-aware-d1-optimization/`, frozen 2026-10-24) and the brief's proposals for the next study. Every
figure quoted below was read from those directories' reports, `results/robust/robust.json`,
`results/diagnostics/diagnostics.json` and the study code under `src/joint-16-etf-d1/`; nothing was recomputed and no
strategy was run. The previous implementation-oriented draft named in the brief
(`CLAUDE_D1_ROBUST_NEIGHBORHOOD_AND_ETF_POOL_DISCOVERY.md`) is **not in the checkout**, so its specific proposals are
critiqued only as the brief restates them (6–12 neighbours for ranking, 32–64 for finalists, a cardinality-calibrated
neighbourhood, simple nested folds).

Legend used throughout: **[fact]** verified in the repository; **[inference]** our reading of those facts;
**[hypothesis]** a claim the new study must test, not assume.

## 1. What the 16-ETF study established, and what it did not

### 1.1 Established facts worth keeping

- **[fact]** The engines are exact and causal to the standard the repository can test: the cached replay equalled the
  full N-book engine in 268 comparisons (1,208 in the previous study), a zero perturbation reproduces the base, and
  a later exit's price or time changes no earlier decision (`tests/joint.test.ts`). The fast path is fixed-pool only;
  every portfolio figure comes from the full engine.
- **[fact]** Under shadow semantics a book's virtual trades depend only on its own symbol, parameters and execution
  settings, so traces are shareable across subsets and the trace key covers every behaviour-relevant input
  (`src/portfolio-aware-d1/trace.ts`). This is what makes a neighbourhood-based search affordable.
- **[fact]** The training landscape of a one-lot shared pool is rough *everywhere*, not only at optimized points: on 12
  configurations nobody optimized (random subsets of random library vectors, 1–16 books), one small step of every
  parameter moved the median net by −$83k to +$55k, with the share of neighbours above the base scattered from 0.02
  to 0.85 (mean ≈ 0.5). For optimized configurations the share above base is 0–12% per member, and the joint median
  is 52–67% of the base (20% for the broad R2).
- **[fact]** Breadth does not pay under one lot: no subset of ≥ 12 books met the $35,000 fixed-pool drawdown; the
  best 9–11-book subsets made ≈ $290k at ≈ $33k drawdown; the best feasible nets by cardinality peak at 4–5 books.
- **[fact]** The leaders are mostly the incumbents' own vectors: the frozen KRE `lowDd` vector is the KRE member of
  nearly every leader (it supplies $160k of R1's $468k), and the XBI/XRT/GDX members are one or two probe steps from
  the frozen `lowDd`/`portfolioAware` vectors; 68% of the top 1,000 discovery configurations contain a control vector
  on its own source symbol.
- **[fact]** R1's advantage over B5 is a 2020 re-timing: of +$79,317, +$75,356 is 2020; the two pools share 40 funded
  lots and differ in which XRT/GDX lots the free pool took in the crash and recovery.
- **[fact]** 2,026 of 36,513 configurations beat B5's training net and 1,092 beat it by ≥ $25,000. The maximum of a
  search this large exceeding B5 by $79k is unremarkable under the null of no true improvement.

### 1.2 What cannot be inferred from the study

- That any configuration, R1 included, is better or worse than B4/B5 out of sample. Nothing new was evaluated after
  training (correctly, under its rules).
- That "no D1 pool is robust" in any general sense. The study measured one neighbourhood definition at one amplitude
  against one universal threshold; it showed the *threshold* is unattainable for every optimized pool, which is a
  statement about the gate as much as about the pools (§2).
- That the rolling-origin correlations (0.61, 0.71) indicate forward skill. The study itself notes the archive was
  generated by a search rewarding the full-training net, so the correlation mostly measures "busy configurations stay
  busy". **[inference]** Any persistence diagnostic computed on a search archive is biased by the search; only
  randomly generated configurations give an unbiased persistence baseline.

## 2. The T6 joint-plateau gate: diagnostic insight versus unjustified cutoff

### 2.1 What T6 did (code: `src/joint-16-etf-d1/robust.ts`, `space.ts` `jointPerturbation`)

For each of 48 draws, every parameter of every member was moved independently by −m, 0 or +m grid steps (m: X 5,
Y 1, stop 5, hold 1, L 5, buffer 5, P 5, W 10), **clamped to the box**, seeded per configuration key. T6 required
≥ 75% of these neighbours feasible (minute DD ≤ $35,000) and P50 ≥ 0.75 × the centre net, plus the one-parameter
probe clauses.

### 2.2 Why the 75% ratio was miscalibrated (not merely strict)

1. **The perturbation mass grows with cardinality.** Each coordinate moves with probability 2/3, so a draw moves on
   average 5.3 coordinates for one book and 26.7 for five. Even for a smooth, non-overfitted objective the expected
   displacement grows with the number of moved coordinates, so a single ratio threshold across 1, 4 and 10 books
   compares unlike quantities. **[fact]** B1 (one book) retained 70%, the four- and five-book pools 53–67%, the
   nine-book R2 20%.
2. **The ratio's denominator is the selected peak.** For a configuration chosen as the argmax of a rough function, the
   neighbourhood median is *expected* to sit well below the centre: the optimizer selected the lucky draw. The
   diagnostics confirm this signature (share of neighbours above base ≈ 0 for optimized pools versus ≈ 0.5 for random
   pools). A ratio gate therefore measures selection intensity, which every optimized configuration has by
   construction; it cannot separate "overfitted" from "selected on a rough but real landscape". The dollar-valued
   neighbourhood median can.
3. **Clamping biases boundary vectors toward stability.** A coordinate at a bound receives "no move" with probability
   2/3 instead of 1/3 (the outward draw is clamped). Several leaders sit at bounds (XBI X = 30.0% at the box ceiling,
   XBI/GDX buffer 0.1% and Y 0.2% near the floor, GDX P 19.1% near the ceiling), so their joint neighbourhoods were
   *narrower* than a mid-box vector's, which flatters them. Reflection at the bounds (§NEIGHBORHOOD) removes this.
4. **A single threshold ignored the denominator problem for small or negative bases**, which the pilot design must
   handle (unoptimized pools with base ≈ 0 give ratios of 5.4 or −0.01 in the diagnostics).
5. **The feasible-share clause (75% of neighbours with DD ≤ $35k) was never calibrated.** B5's 25% and R1's 40% may be
   typical of *any* feasible pool whose drawdown sits at 60–75% of the ceiling, since the drawdown of a one-lot pool
   is a maximum statistic that a re-timed lot can raise sharply. Whether 75% is attainable by unoptimized feasible
   pools is an empirical question the pilot must answer before any threshold is fixed.

### 2.3 Why T6 was nevertheless the most valuable result of the study

- It demonstrated that the one-parameter probe plateau (used to accept B5 in the previous study; medians 95–100%) does
  not discriminate at all: it is passed by every optimized vector because single-coordinate moves rarely re-time a
  funded lot. **[inference]** Any future gate built only on one-coordinate probes is vacuous for this allocator.
- It gave dollar-valued smoothed figures that are far closer to one another than the peaks: B5 $215,130, R1 $246,610,
  R4 $249,932 (peaks $388k, $468k, $372k). **[inference]** If the neighbourhood median is a better estimate of a
  region's typical historical outcome than its peak, the "R1 beats B5 by $79k" headline shrinks to ≈ $31k, below the
  $25k materiality bar once sampling error (≈ $8–10k on 48 draws) is considered — and R4, with a *lower* peak than
  B5, has a *higher* smoothed figure. The ranking by peak and by smoothed value disagree, which is exactly the case in
  which search by peak is the wrong tool.
- Its diagnostics on unoptimized configurations are the correct template for calibration: random pools, stratified by
  cardinality, with the same perturbation machinery.

### 2.4 Decision for the new design

The universal 75% retention ratio **disappears as a gate**. What replaces it (details in NEIGHBORHOOD_AND_OBJECTIVES.md):
the dollar-valued neighbourhood median (and lower quartile) become the **search objective**, so sharp peaks lose to
plateaus inside the search rather than being vetoed afterwards; the retention ratio and the share-above-base are kept
as **descriptive peakedness diagnostics**; neighbourhood risk is expressed in absolute dollars (median and 90th
percentile of neighbour drawdowns) against predeclared ceilings; the only calibrated constants (a materiality margin
and the discovery sample size) are set from random controls by a rule written before the pilot runs.

## 3. Search pressure, the peak-net objective and the "robustness battery afterwards" pattern

- **[fact]** Discovery optimized (−net, minute DD, books) and refinement maximized net − $5,000 × books; the
  neighbourhood was computed only for the 20-configuration shortlist. Every selection pressure therefore pointed at
  peaks, and the plateau test could only veto. Of 36,513 evaluations, 9,916 were local refinement moves around the
  leaders — exactly the moves that climb a peak of a rough function.
- **[fact]** The drawdown objective treated a zero-trade member as free: GLD's inert vectors appeared 2,670 times on
  Pareto fronts; 10.5% of discovery evaluations funded no lot. **[inference]** A multi-objective front with a
  drawdown axis rewards inertness; a scalar smoothed objective with a hard drawdown constraint and an activity floor
  does not.
- **[fact]** Refinement's 92 accepted moves raised every contender's net, and the refined configurations' joint P50
  shares (0.20–0.67) were no better than the contenders' (0.38–0.63). **[inference]** Refinement by peak net buys
  in-sample dollars and no smoothness; refinement must optimize the smoothed objective or not run at all.
- **[fact]** Benchmark-seeded island I8 and two uniform-start islands converged to the same core. **[inference]** This is
  weak evidence of a real training-period attractor at {XBI, XRT, KRE, GDX}, and strong evidence that peak-net search
  on this data has one basin. It says nothing about whether the attractor persists, and the KRE `lowDd` vector (an
  inspected control present in the library) acted as a seed in *every* island through the library.

## 4. The apparent profitability of R1 and B5

- **R1 ($467,806).** Maximum of a 36,513-trial correlated search; 43% of net in 2020; +95% of its edge over B5 is a
  2020 re-timing; three of four members are near-copies of inspected training-optimized vectors; the fourth (KRE) is
  that vector exactly. Its smoothed value ($246,610 ± ≈ $9k) is ≈ $31k above B5's ($215,130) and ≈ $3k below R4's.
  **[inference]** R1 is best read as "the same basin as B5 without VGT", not as a discovery.
- **B5 ($388,489 training; +$55,670 post-training vs B4).** Accepted by the previous study under one-parameter probes
  that discriminate nothing; its post-training gain rests on two GDX lots (without them −$557); its lower portfolio
  drawdown (29.67% vs 34.65%) is one GDX lot held through March 2020. **[inference]** B5 is an optional forward test,
  as its own study said; it is not a bar that a new configuration must clear at its *peak*. A fair benchmark
  comparison is smoothed-versus-smoothed under one neighbourhood definition and one set of random draws.
- **B4 ($242,196).** Fails T7 (VGT's marginal contribution is −$6,827 in training) and has the highest drawdown of the
  three. It remains the default because it is the least optimized of the three, which is a sound reason.

## 5. Folds, selection and the status of 2024–2026

- **[fact]** The study's F1/F2/F3 "folds" were attribution periods of one full-training run, seen by the search; they
  measured path concentration, not forward persistence. The eight-ETF program (`src/multi-etf-d1/context.ts`) did
  have genuine rolling-origin folds for single-symbol vectors, but no study has walk-forward-validated the *pool
  selection procedure*.
- **[fact]** 2024-01-02 → 2026-10-13 has been inspected repeatedly: VGT by the VGT programs, XBI/XRT/KRE by the eight-ETF
  sealed holdout (run once, 2026-10-23), the allocator by the multi-ETF allocator program, GDX/XOP by the
  portfolio-aware evaluation, and every benchmark again by the 16-ETF controls. The decision to study GDX followed a
  screen whose candidates' later history the analyst could know. **[inference]** The window is a *locked historical
  comparison* at best: it can falsify (a candidate that reverses there is not recommended) but cannot validate.
- **[fact]** No bar after 2026-10-13 exists in the repository. Genuine forward evidence begins there.

## 6. Data and accounting assumptions that bias comparisons

- **Distributions.** Fixed mode is price-only for every lot; portfolio mode books VGT/VOOG core and VGT-lot
  distributions but no other lot's (`lotDividends` applies to VGT lots only by the frozen convention). High-yield
  books (EWZ, XLE, XLU, TLT, KRE) are understated against VGT books, and an ex-dividend drop is seen by D1 as a price
  loss that can trigger a buffered-loss or stop exit — a *signal* effect, not only an accounting one. The N-book
  engine has a `lotDividends` switch, so a symmetric portfolio variant (no lot distributions for any book) costs
  nothing to add and gives a fair comparison; the frozen variant must be kept for continuity.
- **Costs.** 0/2/5/10 bps per side model spread, slippage and impact; Vanguard's online ETF commission is zero and is
  not what the cost models. 5 bps is a research assumption, not a measured execution cost. Fill semantics (first
  actual trade after the decision minute; stops at the level or the gapped open) must not change.
- **VOOG.** Tradable under the engine's rules but thin in training (median 134 bars, 311 shares per bar, $16M/day).
  Fills at sparse actual bars are honest to the data and optimistic for size. A capacity *flag* is needed, not a
  silent exclusion.
- **Splits.** Six recorded, forward-adjusted once, verified against the data; XOP's 2020 reverse split lies inside
  training and is handled. A new symbol's unrecorded split would appear as a crash and must be caught by
  `npm run market-events -- verify`'s unexplained-gap listing before any strategy runs.
- **Box edges.** Several leading vectors sit on the box boundary (X at 30.0%, Y and buffer at 0.1–0.2%, P at 19.1%):
  the optimizer "wants" to leave the box, and the engine grid itself caps X at 30.0%. Widening the box after seeing
  this would be tuning after observation; keeping it is a known limitation to report (boundary-sitting coordinates
  are counted and labelled for every finalist).

## 7. Critique of the brief's proposed remedies

| Proposal (as restated in the brief) | Assessment | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 6–12 neighbours for broad ranking, 32–64 for finalists | 6–12 independent draws give a median with standard error ≈ $15–25k on the leaders' spread; selecting the argmax of such a noisy statistic re-creates the winner's curse one level up. Usable only with common random numbers (so comparisons are paired) and a re-evaluation at larger n before any promotion, with the *reported* figure coming from an independent batch. | Adopt with CRN, two-stage re-evaluation and independent confirmation batches (NEIGHBORHOOD §4–§5). |
| A cardinality-calibrated retention threshold | Calibrating the *ratio* per cardinality still selects on a ratio whose denominator is the selected peak. The right normalization is to drop the ratio and compare dollars. | Rejected as a gate; the all-books neighbourhood is kept as the perturbation model and a fixed-mass class is added as the cardinality-neutral diagnostic. |
| Simple nested folds | Necessary but not sufficient: folds validate the *procedure*, and nothing in 2017–2023 is out of sample for the final configuration. Two folds with 1.5–2 years of validation and ≈ 12 funded lots per year are thin; they must be interpreted against random-control baselines, not against B4/B5 (which were fitted on the validation years). | Adopt as the primary time-ordered evidence, with a procedure-level gate and a raw-net ablation (WALK_FORWARD §3–§5). |
| Reuse the 2024–2026 window as a holdout | Inspected repeatedly. | Secondary locked confirmation that can only block. |
| Seed the search with R1/R4 | Re-imports the peak-net search's history and the inspected control vectors; makes "unseeded versus seeded" uninformative. | R1/R4 are inspect-only comparators; a benchmark-seeded arm runs beside the unseeded primary arm for comparison only. |
| Add a handful of ETFs | The 16 were chosen expecting VGT to anchor; a broad, category-based expansion is the only way to test whether different correction mechanisms help a one-lot pool. The cost is breadth of search, handled by budget rules, not by standalone pre-screening. | Adopt a two-tier, data-quality-gated expansion (ETF_UNIVERSE §2–§4). |

## 8. Whether continuing to tune 2016–2023 has remaining statistical value

Honest answer: **limited, and mostly methodological.** The eligible window holds ≈ 6.5 years, one great crash, one
bear market and ≈ 70–90 funded lots for a four- or five-book pool. Tens of thousands of correlated configurations have
already been scored on it, and the current benchmarks' vectors were chosen on it. A further search can:

- test whether a *procedure* that selects on smoothed fitness persists forward inside the window better than the
  peak-net procedure did (the walk-forward ablation) — a methodological result that stands whatever the final
  configuration is;
- find structurally different pools (different correction mechanisms, compact memberships) whose smoothed training
  outcomes are at least comparable to the benchmarks' smoothed outcomes at lower risk — candidates for a forward test;
- establish calibrated, reusable machinery (controls, neighbourhoods, access doors, ingestion) for the only evidence
  that will eventually matter, sessions after 2026-10-13.

It cannot show that the selected system is better. The protocol therefore phrases its best possible outcome as
"primary forward-test candidate, robustly positive on smoothed training fitness, procedure-level persistence shown in
k of 2 folds, historically consistent on the inspected 2024–2026 window", and keeps "no advancement" as a first-class
outcome.
