# Root cause: why Set 2 stayed out of the 2023 recovery

Every number here is generated by `npm run recovery-eligibility -- root-cause` (tables in
`results/diagnostics/root-cause.md`, the full session trace in `results/diagnostics/root-cause-trace.csv`) on
`data/VGT/VGT-2016-2025-1-minute.csv` (2,459 sessions, 2016-01-04 → 2025-10-13). The engine reproduces the
file exactly: $150,630 net, $21,183 minute-sampled drawdown ($16,577 daily), 13 closed trades plus one open,
$20,855 interest, trade for trade (`results/diagnostics/root-cause.json`; the file's own run is
reproduced in IMPLEMENTATION_AUDIT.md).

## 1. How the reference high works

`src/app/allIn-trailingBuy-bufferedLoss-noReentry.ts` computes, for every session, `recentHigh` as the
highest session high of the **previous 74 completed sessions**, never including the current session
(`sessions.slice(index - 74, index)`). It is recomputed every session; nothing is ever frozen except inside a
setup: when a completed minute closes at or below `recentHigh × (1 − 0.216)` with no position and no setup,
the setup records that `recentHigh` and the trailing low starts at the activating close. The setup then
persists across sessions until the 0.3% rebound buys; it never needs the 21.6% condition again. After a fill
the setup is discarded. After an exit the next setup needs a **fresh** 21.6% close below the **current**
rolling high. There is no episode memory of any kind, no re-entry restriction, and an exit session never
arms. Warm-up only matters before session 74.

Three facts follow:

1. **With L 74 the correction was invisible.** The deepest point of the whole 2023 window relative to the
   rolling high was 14.1%, and in the December–January trough only 13.0%: the threshold was missed by 7.5
   percentage points, not by a hair.
2. **Even a frozen reference would not have been enough at 21.6%.** Keeping the November setup's own
   reference high (391.60) alive, the January 6 low was 20.76% below it: 0.84 points short. The persistent
   reference that would have worked is the *previous* episode's anchor, the December 2021 peak of 467.06,
   which the strategy had already used in March 2022 and which a 74-session window forgot in April 2022.
3. **Trades2 caught 2023 by 0.76 points.** Its X 20.0% against an L 104 reference of 391.60 needed 313.28;
   the December 28 minute closes reached 310–312. Set 2's own X at L 104 needs 307.01 and misses. The
   Trades2 participation was a near-coincidence of two parameters, not a design property.

## 5. Why `allIn.ts` entered on January 4, 2023

`allIn.ts` decides once per session on the 15:59 minute's close against a **90-session** reference high
with a **10%** threshold. On 2023-01-04 its reference was 377.61 (the 2022-08-25 high, still inside 90
sessions), its level 339.85, and its decision price 317.46, a 15.9% decline: well past 10%. Separating the
two differences through file 4's own architecture, one change at a time (file semantics, arming from
session L; `results/diagnostics/root-cause.md`):

The **10% threshold alone** is sufficient to make file 4 trade the window (at L 74 the December 19 close of
323.08 is 10.5% below 357.47); the lookback difference adds 2 points of measured depth and is not necessary.
The threshold, however, is not a free change: at 10% the architecture (8-day hold, 2.9% buffer, 0.3%
rebound) makes 54 entries, 33 losses and a $49,082 drawdown. `allIn.ts` survives its 10% because it enters
immediately at the close and holds 15 days; with those settings its own 2023 trade earned $27,611 and its
full-file result is $169,090 at a $32,084 minute drawdown (33 closed trades plus one open). Note also that
Set 2's 7.3% stop would have closed a December 28 entry on 2023-03-13 (+$8,177, trade "X 20.0% and L 104"
above); Trades2's 7.8% stop survived that March pullback by a few dollars and rode to August (+$29,374).
Both the entry and the hold through the February–March pullback were marginal.

## 6. Necessary versus contributing causes

- **Necessary:** the arming rule measures depth only against a rolling 74-session high. After a long decline
  with successively lower rallies, the window forgets the real correction's peak within a few months, the
  measured depth collapses (from 21.7% on November 4 to 13% on December 28 for the *same* price level), and
  a recovery that starts from the correction's trough can never satisfy a 21.6% test. This is a general
  correction-memory property of the design, not an accident of 2022: the same thing would happen after any
  bear market longer than the window.
- **Contributing:** the 21.6% threshold is deep enough that even a frozen reference from the last rally
  (391.60) fell short by 0.84 points; the 7.3% stop would have cut a late-December entry in March 2023; and
  the no-re-entry architecture has no post-exit state at all, so the profitable December 15 exit left
  nothing behind to act on the subsequent 6% pullback.
- **Not causes:** pending orders, session restrictions, warm-up, the buffered exit, or any execution
  difference with `allIn.ts`. The entry logic was simply never eligible.
- **Is it a bug or a trade-off?** It is a deliberate trade-off that the earlier optimization made for a
  reason: the same decay that hid 2023 is what kept Set 2 out of the eleven losing bear-market re-entries
  that a persistent reference produces in 2022 (LOOKBACK_CONTROLS.md). The question the rest of this program
  answers is whether a bounded memory can keep the first without the second.
