controls` (`results/diagnostics/controls.md`, the per-session sweeps in `results/diagnostics/lookback-sweep.csv`
and `threshold-sweep.csv`). "File semantics" arms from session L, exactly as the strategy file does; "common
run" arms from session 365 (2017-06-15), the eligibility session used for every engine evaluation in this
program. For Set 2 the two coincide (its first trade is 2018-12-21); they only differ for shallow thresholds.

Set 2 at L 74 made seven 2022 trades for +$27,337 with a worst trade of −$1,092; L 365 makes fourteen for
−$6,036 in 2022 (eleven of its 2022 entries lose, including the −$12,022 of June 8, an entry at 364 one
week before the June low), and its 2023 participation (+$19,323 over four trades, two of them losses) does
not pay for the drawdown they create. The drawdown of $35,099 runs from
2022-03-29 to 2022-10-13 and is entirely this sequence. L 183 behaves the same way until October 2022 and
then its reference decays (to 398.52 by January 2023), so it catches only the first +$9,022 of 2023.

So the persistence that would have recognized the 2023 recovery is the same persistence that re-arms on
every failed bear-market rally. This is the trade-off a bounded mechanism has to beat (COUNTERFACTUALS.md).

Lowering X globally is the "simple" control the brief asked for, and it fails on every axis: the only
thresholds that trade the 2023 window (≤ 12%) carry $46–54k drawdowns and 28–52 losing trades, and the
intermediate thresholds (14–21%) are worse than 21.6% without reaching 2023 at all. The sharp step between
21.0% ($121,794 / $31,989) and 21.6% ($150,630 / $21,183) is one trade: at 21.0% the February 2020 session
arms a week earlier and the −$11,837 of a pre-crash entry is added. Set 2's neighbourhood above 21.6% is
smooth (22–24%: $141–147k at $21.2–21.4k), which is why the optimizer liked it.
