## Questions to resolve

1. Did the failed historical selections have useful alternatives in their cutoff archives? If so, were the alternatives lost at screening, qualification, promotion or final ranking?
2. Could information genuinely available at that cutoff distinguish useful alternatives, or do they look attractive only after seeing the next period?
3. Were development neighborhoods largely reproducing the same few profitable episodes? Did sparse decision rules, clipping, inactive genes or duplicate ledgers make stability appear stronger than it was?
4. Do any of at most three simple, predeclared policy changes improve subsequent-period selections repeatedly, with acceptable profit and risk trade-offs?
5. Is there enough evidence to propose a process change, or should we retain the current process with stronger limitations, reduce further optimization, or commission a different narrowly defined investigation?

A candidate found in hindsight is an **oracle diagnostic**, not an actionable selection policy. No finite archive can prove that no useful phenotype exists outside it. Shared market periods across studies are correlated evidence, not independent replications.

## Stage 2 — at most three frozen policy alternatives

Use the unchanged original policy as the reference, and include already-archived robust/balanced role sequences as contextual controls without presenting them as new discoveries.

Choose **no more than three** simple hypotheses after the mechanism audit. Suitable families are:

- Severity-aware selection: does a development selection-neighborhood severity measure add useful information beyond the existing p90 gate?
- Subperiod consistency: does accounting for development results across distinct chronological subperiods reduce dependence on one market regime?
- Episode sensitivity: does accounting for development profit after withholding new funding in the leading episode identify less brittle candidates?

These are options, not an instruction to force all three. Freeze one formula per chosen family, exact scaling/weights/tie-breaks, applicable metrics, missing-data treatment, qualification and fallback, plus the shortlist and reporting rules. Do not search a grid of weights, thresholds, policy combinations or “best two out of three.” No automatic trade-count floor, concentration veto, named-event ban or mandatory trading during every year. Include the profit sacrificed and useful candidates rejected by each alternative.

Keep source-study qualification gates intact for the main matched comparison; experimental formulas change ranking among eligible candidates. Explain that original screening/search may already have discarded alternatives, which archive reselection cannot undo. Any proposed change to search promotion or eligibility belongs in the recommendations, not an unannounced second experiment.

Compute newly required selection features on training doors only, with separate seeds from held-back confirmation. Reusing confirmation statistics to choose a new winner contaminates that confirmation; if that happens in a descriptive analysis, label it and obtain fresh confirmation for the frozen policy-selected candidate before claiming qualification. Do not repeatedly resample until it qualifies. Freeze how a failure is handled, including a no-selection outcome where appropriate.

Policies must be executable using information available at each training cutoff. Do not use subsequent losses, idle intervals, future entry dates, published validation profits or C24 settings to choose earlier-fold candidates. Differences in training length must be addressed by a single declared normalization rather than cutoff-specific tuning.

## Stage 3 — matched archive reselection and the discovery diagnostic

Apply every frozen policy to the same eligible archive universe at each subject/cutoff. If full feature computation exceeds the budget, freeze a common, development-only cohort before opening new next-period outcomes: include original selections, the original shortlist, and deterministic stratified samples from promotion stages, profit ranks and structural groups. Use up to **96 distinct candidates per subject/cutoff** for newly simulated next-period diagnostic outcomes. Declare exactly what was excluded. Do not choose the cohort using its subsequent profit.

Two analyses must remain separate:

1. **Realizable policy test:** compare each rule's chosen configuration against the original choice, using training information only. Report next-period net/DD, paired deltas, drawdown breaches, uninvested periods, and sequential results across 2020–2024.
2. **Hindsight availability diagnostic:** after choices are sealed, inspect next-period outcomes of the frozen cohort. Did useful alternatives exist in that evaluated set? Report their prevalence and original promotion/ranking positions, rather than showcasing only the best future winner. The hindsight frontier is not a selectable strategy and not an achievable improvement estimate.

A better alternative existing in the archive demonstrates opportunity, not predictability. No successful alternative in a sampled cohort means “not found in this cohort,” not “the search space contains none.” Do not call a new ranking a better complete optimizer: the original adaptive search was guided by its old objectives. Reselection tests only the final policy conditional on the archived discoveries; validating a changed search itself would need a later separately authorized experiment.

Use the source studies' verified continuous sequential-policy transition semantics. Reconcile transitions, costs and open positions; do not concatenate incompatible reset equity curves or sum drawdowns. Report fold results and complete sequential net/DD for every rule, including failures. Provide a compact attribution of changed trades and exposure.

Freeze a conservative “merits further confirmation” decision rule before these results, covering repeated improvement across different subsequent periods, the unchanged risk budget, profit sacrificed and inconsistency across the two subjects. Do not invent a requirement to dominate on every noisy statistic. Describe uncertainty and correlated evidence; with three historical slices, do not promise statistically established superiority.

## Hard bounds and stopping

- **Zero new strategy-parameter search proposals.** Candidate configurations come from the appropriate existing cutoff archives.
- **At most three new selection formulas**, plus the original policy and previously available descriptive controls.
- **At most 100,000 fresh backend simulations in total**, including feature computation, confirmation, stresses, diagnostics and forward evaluations. Count archive hits separately.
- Within that total, **at most 800 fresh next-slice/full-history simulations**. The cohort cap above plus frozen policy evaluations must fit this limit.

- Stop after the declared comparisons, at a resource/data blocker that prevents valid inference, or under the frozen futility rule. No “one more promising tweak.” A discovered new hypothesis goes into the report for later review.
