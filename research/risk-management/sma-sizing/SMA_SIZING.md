# Experiment 1: SMA200-based position sizing (`sma_sizing`)

**Preliminary classification: Inconclusive.**

The rule cuts the maximum drawdown by 20–37% on every dataset. It also beats a matched-exposure control on drawdown in all four, though in three of them by only 3.5–7.7%.

However, the size of the selected trades does not look special. Halving an arbitrary set of the same number of trades does at least as well against its own control in 15–38% of cases on drawdown and 28–70% on profit. The rule also halves the first entry after every major bottom except 2020, so recovery-window P&L falls to 36–85% of the baseline outside 2020. Net profit falls 12–21% in both 2016–2026 datasets.

All numbers come from `results/summary.json` and `results/analysis.json`. Tables are regenerated by `analyze.py`.

## 1. Configuration and exact implementation

### Configs

| Config | Rule |
|---|---|
| `A_baseline` | Unmodified baseline: $100,000 on every entry. It subclasses `Strategy` and overrides `entry_size` only to log each entry, returning `e.params.base_size` unchanged. |
| `B_bearish_sma200_sizing` | Overrides only `entry_size`. All other hooks are inert. |

For an entry at session *i* (0-based index into the full file):

- `SMA200_i` is the mean of `close[i−199 … i]`. Session *i*'s own close is included; it is the entry price, known at the decision.
- `SMA200_{i−20}` is the mean of `close[i−219 … i−20]`.
- **Bearish** means `close_i < SMA200_i` **and** `SMA200_i < SMA200_{i−20}`. Both inequalities are strict, so a tie counts as not bearish (none occurred).
- **Size** is $50,000 if bearish, otherwise $100,000.
- **Fallback:** if `SMA200_{i−20}` is unavailable (*i* < 219), the size is $100,000 and the entry is counted as a fallback. This also applies when `SMA200_i` alone exists (199 ≤ *i* < 219): QQQ on 2000-01-05, *i* = 208.
- The size is fixed for the life of the position. There are no add-ons and no resizing when the regime changes while a trade is open.

Code:
- `strategies.py`: the rule, using only `e.c[:i+1]`.
- `run.py`: calls `metrics.run_config` for each (config, dataset), adds the experiment `extra`, and calls `write_summary`.
- `verify.py`: verification checks.
- `analyze.py`: report tables and attribution.

### Chronological order within session *i* (frozen engine, unchanged)

1. **Before the open**, if a position opened in an earlier session is held, the trailing stop is active only if a sale at the stop would be profitable after interest accrued through session *i*. The stop level is 8% below the highest high through session *i−1* (the entry close on the first session) and never moves down.
2. **At the open**, one fill only:
   - the 15-calendar-day loss exit sells if a sale at the open would lose money;
   - otherwise, an active stop that the open gaps through fills at the open.
3. **During the session**, an active stop fills at its level if the low reaches it.
4. **At the close**, if no position was held at the start of the session (so never on an exit session):
   - the signal fires when `close_i` is ≥ 10% below the highest high of sessions *i−90 … i−1*;
   - the engine then calls `entry_size(e, i)`, and **this experiment's only intervention happens here**: SMA200_i and SMA200_{i−20} are computed from closes 0…*i*, and the size is chosen;
   - the engine buys `size / close_i` shares (fractional) at `close_i`.
5. If the position survived the session, the post-entry high and the stop are updated.
6. The position is marked to market at the close, net of interest accrued at 11%/yr simple on the amount borrowed, per calendar day.

### Assumptions

- Prices are the split- and dividend-adjusted values in the data files, used as given.
- The full file is used for each dataset.
- Session *i* = 0 is the first row of the file. The engine never enters on session 0.
- Execution is market-on-close at exactly the close used in the SMA decision. This is the same idealisation as the baseline: a live system would have to decide moments before the close.
- Only the amount borrowed changes. Interest, P&L and margin dollar-days all scale with size.
- No look-ahead: the decision reads closes 0…*i* only. A truncation test confirms this (section 2).

### A structural property used throughout

The trailing-stop activation and the loss exit depend only on the **sign** of P&L, so position size never changes the trade sequence. B therefore has exactly the baseline's trades, and each trade's P&L and interest equal the baseline's × size / $100,000 (verified to within 1e-12).

Every difference between A and B, and between B and its matched control (a uniformly scaled baseline), is therefore a reweighting of the same trades. The difference can be attributed exactly, trade by trade.

## 4. Matched-exposure comparison

The control is a complete baseline run at a fixed size X with margin dollar-days equal to B's (`metrics.matched_control`). Because the control is a uniformly scaled baseline, **its profit/DD always equals the baseline's**. So "B beats its control on profit/DD" is the same statement as "B's profit/DD exceeds the baseline's".

- Max drawdown is below the control in 4 of 4 datasets.

## 9. Preliminary classification: **Inconclusive**

### Historical observations (data only)

- SMA200 lags. It was still rising during the first 6–8 months of the 2000 decline, during November 2007 – January 2008, and during January – April 2022. It was still falling at the March 2009, October 2002 / March 2003, January 2023 and April 2025 bottoms.
- The February–March 2020 crash was too fast for SMA200 to turn down.
- Ten-percent dip signals cluster in downtrends. The bearish regime covers 10–24% of sessions but 27–65% of entries.

### Backtest calculations (this engine, these data)

- B has the same trades as the baseline, with 12–68 of them halved.
- Max DD falls 19.5–36.7%, and is 3.5–14.9% below the matched control in all four datasets.
- Net profit vs control: +2,681, +13,573, −6,889 and +4,182. Profit/DD vs control: +0.09, +0.14, −0.07 and +0.38.
- Net profit vs baseline: −2.5%, +$14,460, −20.8% and −12.3%.
- Recovery windows retain 36–85% of the baseline P&L outside 2020.
- In the chance check, 15–38% of arbitrary halvings of the same number of trades match B's drawdown improvement against their own controls, and 28–70% match its profit gain.

### Hypotheses (not tested here)

- Halving entries in confirmed downtrends trims the long chains of 15-day loss exits in prolonged bear markets. This would explain the QQQ 2000–2002 result, the only window where the loss fell by more than the exposure did.
- A lagging trend filter will tend to halve the first rebound entry after V-shaped bottoms. Those entries are where this dip-buying strategy makes much of its money.

### Inferences (from the calculations, with the stated caveats)

- Most of the drawdown reduction is explained by deleveraging. The residual benefit is consistent in sign but small (3.5–8% of control DD in three of four datasets) and within what arbitrary selections produce.
- The rule's profit effect depends on where one or two bottom-recovery trades fall relative to a near-zero SMA200 slope. That is a fragile basis for a risk rule.
- In the 2016–2026 data the rule reduced risk mostly at the expense of recovery profit. Its residual benefit there, against a control, was mixed: −6,889 for VGT and +4,182 for QQQ.

### Limitations

- **Not independent evidence.** The four datasets are two overlapping pairs of highly correlated ETFs (VGT and QQQ share most entry dates in 2016–2026). The evidence amounts to roughly two histories and three or four bear episodes.
- **Few halved trades** in 2016–2026 (12 per dataset), and the results are dominated by 2–3 trades.
- **In-sample** results, with one predefined rule. The chance check is descriptive: trades are not independent, and it is not a formal significance test.
- **Idealised execution:** market-on-close at the same close used in the decision, no slippage or commissions, fractional shares, a constant 11% rate, adjusted prices.
- **Drawdown definition:** measured in dollars from a cumulative-P&L peak starting at 0, not account equity.
- **Warm-up:** the warm-up fallback (5 entries) is unclassifiable without data from before each file starts.

## 10. Parameters to flag for a later, separately authorised robustness study (not run)

1. **Slope lookback (20 sessions) and slope threshold (strictly < 0).** Many decisive classifications sit within ±0.4% of zero slope (Q2), so this is the parameter most likely to swing results.
2. **SMA length (200).**
3. **Regime definition:** close < SMA AND falling slope, against either condition alone. The slope condition is the binding one in practice.
4. **Bearish size ($50k) and the two-level step**, against graduated sizing.
5. **Decision timing:** using close_i (the execution price) against close_{i−1} or an intraday estimate, as a live system would need.
6. **Regime evaluated at entry only**, against re-evaluation while a position is open (would need `add_on` or exit hooks, so a different experiment).
7. **Warm-up handling** using data from before each file starts, to classify the 5 fallback entries.
8. **Out-of-sample instruments and periods** (other ETFs in `data/` were out of scope here).
