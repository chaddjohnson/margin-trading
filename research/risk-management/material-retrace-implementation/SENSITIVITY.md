# Sensitivity — material-loss cutoff × retracement fraction (production fixed at 0.5% / 3% / 38%)

A predefined 4 × 5 grid — cutoff {0.25%, **0.5%**, 0.75%, 1%} × retrace {0.34, **0.38**, 0.42, 0.46, 0.50},
3% lower-price re-entry fixed — registered in `registry.jsonl` before evaluation and run through the
frozen framework on the four frozen datasets and on VGT 2016 → 2025-12-31 (`reference_runs.py`;
tables in `results/analysis/sensitivity.md`, generated by `analyze_sensitivity.py`). This is a
stability measurement; **the production parameters are not changed and no optimum is declared.** The
grid was examined after the production point was fixed; it is in-sample on the same data as everything
before it.

## 1. VGT 2016 → 2025-12-31 (the primary period)

(net profit; max DD is 28,687 at every point with cutoff ≤ 0.75% and retrace ≤ 0.46, 28,208 on the 1%
row, 29,336 at retrace 0.50; previous 3% rule 136,666 / 28,687.)

- **Smoothness.** 19 of 20 points beat the previous 3% rule. Along the retrace axis at the production
  cutoff the surface is a plateau for 0.34–0.42 (167.8k–172.9k; ±5k) and falls gently at 0.46/0.50
  (164.7k/157.2k) as the reset becomes slower and the frozen-high-style sidelining returns. The
  production point is the highest point of its row by a $1.6k–$5k margin — a shoulder, not a spike.
- **Cutoff axis.** The 0.5% and 0.75% rows are **trade-identical** on every dataset: no historical
  trade has a price return between −0.75% and −0.5%, so the effective choice is a band, not a line.
  The 1% row is uniformly +$4,285 (the 2024-09-04 −0.78% loss becomes exempt; one trade). The 0.25%
  row is uniformly worse because the −0.25% cutoff arms on the 2022-11-03 loss (−0.49% price) that the
  production row exempts: at retrace 0.38 that costs one trade (2022-11-14 → 11-29 −$1,749 replaces
  2022-11-04 → 12-16 +$4,640; the January-2023 trade is still caught because the reset fires in time)
  — a $6.4k gap. The 0.25% row also contains the grid's only discontinuity, **$40,705 between
  (0.25%, 0.42) and (0.25%, 0.46)**: with the slower reset, (i) the 2018-11-07 reset no longer fires,
  so the December-2018 path becomes 2018-11-20 (−$87) → 2018-12-11 (−$11,539, the Christmas-Eve crash)
  instead of 11-12/12-17 (−$3,532/−$3,631) → 01-22 (+$11,183), and (ii) after the extra November-2022
  arming the reset comes too late, so 2022-12-01 (−$5,861) and 2022-12-22 (−$2,097) replace the
  2022-11-04 (+$4,640) and **2023-01-04 (+$28,006)** entries. The production row never arms on the
  November-2022 loss and shows no such cliff anywhere on the grid.
- **Single-trade dependence.** Excluding each configuration's largest winner (the +$48,014 April-2020
  trade everywhere) leaves 109k–125k on the production row and preserves the ordering; the row's
  improvement over the previous rule does not depend on that trade. It does depend on the
  January-2023 trade (+$28,006), which every point with cutoff ≥ 0.5% captures and which sits 0.0076pp
  from the 0.5% cutoff (VGT_2016_2025.md §3).
- **Bear-market cost.** The 2022 window is −$165 on every row with cutoff ≥ 0.5% and −$6.6k to −$9.9k
  on the 0.25% row; no neighbor of the production point materially increases cumulative bear losses.
- **Exposure.** Dollar-days ratio 0.863–0.869 across retrace 0.34–0.46 at the production cutoff; the
  net advantage over the matched-exposure control stays +$45k to +$53k across the plateau and the
  drawdown advantage −$4.4k to −$4.6k. The differences between neighbors are decision differences, not
  leverage differences.

## 3. Robustness verdicts (the brief's questions)

1. **Do results change smoothly across neighbors?** On VGT (both periods): yes along the retrace axis
   between 0.34 and 0.46 and across cutoffs 0.5–1%; the 0.25% row is the exception. On QQQ 1999–2015:
   no — retrace 0.38 is a $19k–$29k peak over both neighbors. On QQQ 2016–2026: small moves (±$5k),
   except the 1% row.
2. **Does a particular trade cause a large discontinuity?** Yes: VGT (0.25% row) the Nov/Dec-2018 and
   Dec-2022 chains, $40.7k; QQQ 1999–2015 the 2004–05 post-recovery chain, $28.9k; QQQ 2016–2026 the
   2022-09-14/15 one-day entry shift between the 0.75% and 1% rows, $19.7k. Each is one changed entry
   that re-phases the rest of the sequence.
3. **Which episodes explain the differences?** Dec 2018, Jan 2023 and Sep 2024 (VGT); 2003–2005 and
   2000–02 (QQQ old); Sep 2022 (QQQ new). All are the episodes already identified in the adaptive-
   reentry program as decided by sub-1% price margins.
4. **Do improvements survive excluding the most favourable episode?** On VGT 2016–2025 excluding the
   largest winner keeps every grid point far above the previous rule's equivalently-reduced figure;
   excluding the January-2023 trade instead would remove most of the improvement over the previous
   3% rule (the retrace plateau's +$30k–36k margin becomes roughly +$2k–8k). On VGT 2005–2015 nothing
   survives excluding the 2009 trade, for any strategy.
5. **Does a nearby threshold materially increase cumulative bear-market losses?** Retrace 0.34 on
   QQQ 1999–2015: yes (+$16.3k more loss in 2000–02). The 1% cutoff on QQQ 2016–2026: yes (2022 window
   roughly doubles). The 0.25% cutoff on VGT: moderately (2022: −$6.6k to −$9.9k vs −$165). The
   production point is not adjacent to a bear-market cliff on VGT; it is on QQQ 1999–2015 (0.34).
6. **Does a parameter change simply reduce margin exposure?** No. Exposure moves little across the
   plateau (0.86–0.87 on VGT; 0.76–0.86 on QQQ old), and the net advantage over the matched-exposure
   control moves with the net result itself. Where results fall (retrace 0.42/0.46 on QQQ old),
   exposure also falls — those points are both worse and less invested, i.e. the sidelining cost,
   not a deleveraging benefit.

**Overall:** on VGT the chosen parameters sit on a plateau (retrace 0.34–0.42 at any cutoff from 0.5% to
1%) whose edges are a −0.25% cutoff and a 0.50 retrace; on QQQ 1999–2015 the retrace fraction is a
knife edge in both directions and the cutoff preference (1% better) is the reverse of QQQ 2016–2026
(0.5% better). The parameters are reasonably stable for the primary instrument and period and
genuinely sensitive on the oldest dataset. Nothing here justifies changing them; it justifies not
trusting any single-dataset improvement of less than ~$10k.
