# Interpretation

This is historical evidence for one path, 2016 to 2025, an unusually strong decade for technology stocks.
It is not a forecast.

## What the strategy added

- Over the period, adding the 20%-of-NAV margin strategy to the $400,000 VOOG/VGT account **raised the
  ending value from $2,379,451 to $2,794,800: +$415,349, or +17.5% of the core-only result**. The CAGR rose
  from 20.0% to 22.0% a year.
- **About $124k of that is the still-open April 2025 lot's market value in excess of the account's total
  debt**, so it would change with VGT's price before any sale. With netted financing, where profits sitting
  in cash pay down the margin loan, the uplift would have been **$513,188**: the brief's rule of keeping profits as idle cash while paying 11% on new debit cost about
  $98k.

## What it cost in risk

- **Deeper dollar drawdowns.**
  - The largest account drawdown grew from $515,698 to $626,006 (+$110,307), in the 2025 sell-off.
  - Minute by minute it grew from $579,298 to $712,964.
  - In percentage terms the worst drawdown was about the same (33.3% against 33.9%, both in 2022).
- **Leverage was modest and stable:** at most 1.22× gross exposure and 24% debt-to-equity. Equity never fell
  below 82% of securities.
- **Maintenance risk was remote:** reaching even a hypothetical 50% requirement would have taken a 64% one-day
  fall in both ETFs at the worst point. That depends on Vanguard's actual house requirements, which weren't
  verified.
- **Financing was material:** $164,299 of interest over the period (about $78k under netted financing).
- **No diversification:** with a lot open, the strategy's P&L had a 0.987 correlation with the core. It is
  more of the same technology bet, timed by dips, financed at 11%.

## Is the size rule manageable on this evidence?

Mostly yes. Twenty percent of NAV kept total debt between 20% and 24% of equity and gross exposure near
1.2×, far from any plausible maintenance call, in all four stress periods (2018, 2020, 2022, 2025).

Things to weigh before relying on it:

1. **Dollar losses scale with the account.** The 2025 trades were about $400k each, so the two 15-day
   losses in March and April 2025 cost $6–14k each, and the account had to absorb them during the core's own 25% fall.
2. **Residual debt and idle cash.** Under the specified rule, losses leave debit outstanding while profits
   sit in cash. A real account may net them automatically; if not, that is about $98k of avoidable
   interest over this period.
3. **Concentration.** The account becomes more VGT-heavy exactly when VGT has fallen 10%. A broker could
   impose higher requirements on a concentrated technology position. The historical cushion leaves room,
   but the exposure is real.
4. **The edge depends on recoveries.** Most of the gain came from a few trades bought into dips that
   recovered: 2020-04-02 (+52%), 2023-01-04 (+34%), and the open 2025-04-15 lot (+44%). A prolonged decline
   without a recovery would turn the strategy's loss exits and growing residual debit into a pure cost on
   top of the core's losses. This decade did not test that.

No parameter, filter or rule was changed or tuned for these results.
