# Study A — standalone GDX D1: results

Fixed $100,000 per entry, 5 bps per side, 11% simple interest, books arming 2017-06-15; every figure comes from the
sealed records under `results/` (tables generated by `scripts/report_tables.py`). Labels: **DEV** = search window
2017-06-15 → 2021-12-31; **VAL** = within-study validation 2022–2023 (not unseen data: 2016–2023 was the training
window of every earlier study); **FX** = 2024-01-02 → 2026-10-13, **previously exposed** retrospective stress test,
opened once after the freeze; **RECENT** = the 12 appended sessions 2026-10-14 → 2026-10-29, never read before, far too
short to validate anything. All period figures are attributions inside one continuous run unless marked "isolated".

## Answer

**No GDX standalone phenotype is nominated.** Under the frozen gates (PROTOCOL.md §5.6) none of the 32 shortlisted
vectors passed G2: for every one of them more than 10% of its ±2-step neighbours (128-point Latin hypercube) breach
the $35,000 minute-drawdown ceiling over 2017-06-15 → 2023-12-29. 29 of 32 also breach the ceiling at their own centre
once 2022 is included (G1), and 11 lose money or have a negative neighbourhood median in VAL (G3). The four controls,
including the B5 GDX book, fail as well. Three vectors failed **only** G2; by amendment A1 (reporting only, written
before the final door opened) they receive full diagnostics below and runnable research commands, labelled **not
nominated**. They are essentially one parameter region: X ≈ 26%, L ≈ 215–228 sessions, stop ≈ 6.2%, hold 5 days,
P 7.6%, W 12–14 — next to the earlier standalone GDX representative (X 27.8, L 231, hold 3), which two independent
searches therefore reached.

The search itself shows strong selection bias: the shortlist's median DEV net of $40,925 per year fell to $3,345 per
year in VAL; the probability of backtest overfitting (CSCV over 14 half-years) is 0.49; the WF20 replication (search
2017–2019, test 2020–2021) found no vector passing the gates and its shortlist's median fell from $24,639/yr to
−$2,553/yr. GDX D1 profits are dominated by March–April 2020 (about 54% of the near-misses' full-history net comes
from 2020).

## 4. March 2020: why the 16–18 March exit happened (diagnostic only)

{{table:studyA_march2020.md}}

All GDX books in this table bought the same minute: **2020-03-16 09:46 at $17.12**, on the Y% rebound from the
running low; the session's low ($16.18) came five minutes later (the lot's worst mark, −$4,896, at 09:53). GDX
closed 3/16 at $21.89 and rose to **$25.36 on 3/17 (11:12)**. D1's trailing stop is profit-conditioned: once the lot's
net is positive the stop trails the highest price by the stop percentage — $25.36 × (1 − 7.1%) = **$23.56** for the
B5 GDX book, × (1 − 6.2%) = **$23.79** for the near-misses. On **3/18** GDX opened at $24.12 and traded through the
stop at 09:30–09:36: exit at the stop, **+$37.4k (B5 book) / +$38.7k (near-misses)** on that lot. GDX then fell to
$20.59 by 15:38 (−18.8% from the 3/17 high), so the exit avoided a further 12.5% fall that day. After the profitable
exit the post-exit continuation (pullback P within W sessions) re-armed and re-entered on 3/19, 3/23 and 3/30–4/1;
the whole March–April sequence made **+$96.4k** for the B5 GDX book (five lots) and +$97–$98k for the near-misses
(the giveback from the 3/17 close to the exit was ≈ 7% of price on the main lot). The book held the slot for most of
16 March → 15 April. Candidates were not ranked by this episode, but every profitable GDX region found by the search
relies on it.
